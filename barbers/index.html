Barber & salon

Barber and salon sites for the ten minutes before

Nobody plans a haircut a fortnight out. They decide they need one, look at a phone, and pick somewhere in about ninety seconds. The site’s whole job is to answer two questions inside that window: what does it cost, and how do I get in.

Talk about your shop
What it costs

Barber and salon decisions happen in the moment. Someone is already out, decides they need a cut, and searches on a phone for somewhere nearby. That shapes everything: the site has to load on mobile data, show services and prices immediately, and make getting in a couple of taps rather than a phone call during someone else’s appointment.

It also has to look like your shop. A barber’s style is the product, and a generic template that could belong to any shop on any high street quietly undercuts the thing you are actually selling.

A search like barber near me open now is answered in under two minutes, on a phone, by someone standing outside somewhere else. Price and availability decide it — and a site that makes either of them hard to find loses to the one next door.

Questions

Straight answers

Should I put my prices on the site?

Almost always yes. Publishing prices removes a comparison step for the customer and a phone call for you. It loses the enquiries that were never going to book at your rate and wins the ones that arrive already comfortable with it.

List the service, the length and anything that changes the price, because that is where the awkward conversations at the till come from.

Do I need online booking, or is a phone number enough?

It depends on how the chair fills. A walk-in shop often does better with a prominent phone number, live opening hours and a clear idea of the wait. An appointment-led salon usually earns its keep with real booking, because the bookings arrive outside the hours you would answer the phone.

We will tell you which we think fits before you pay for the more expensive one.

Can you match the site to my shop’s branding?

Yes, and in this trade it matters more than in most. The style of the shop is a large part of what a customer is choosing between, so the site should read as your shop rather than as a theme with your logo dropped into the corner.

Will it help me show up in local searches?

It gives you a genuine chance. The build handles the structural half — valid local business markup, opening hours, consistent location signals and a site that is fast on a phone.

The other half is your Google Business Profile and your review volume, which for a barber shop is usually seen more often than the website itself. Nobody can guarantee a ranking, and Google’s own guidance says to treat anyone who does as a warning sign.

How long does it take?

Typically two to four weeks, depending on whether the photographs already exist. Booking integration is scoped alongside it. You get a timeline with the quote, before anything begins.
