Transport

Taxi and private hire sites that get booked

The national apps own the app stores and the paid ads. What they do not own is the passenger who searches for a taxi in your town and expects a bookable local option in seconds — and that search is winnable.

Talk about your firm
What it costs

A firm like yours already has a strong base of repeat customers with the number saved in their phone. That trade is not the opportunity — it is already yours. The opportunity is the passenger who does not have your number: the one searching for a taxi in your town, who will default to a national app out of habit unless a credible local option appears first.

Two things win that passenger. Being findable at the moment they search, and looking established enough that a stranger is willing to get in the car. Both are things a site can do, and neither of them needs an app.

A search like taxi Southampton is made by someone who wants a ride now and has no loyalty yet. Whoever they can book fastest, with the least doubt about who they are booking, gets the fare.

The search that decides it

The job

What the site has to do

A first-time passenger is making two decisions at once: whether to book you, and whether to trust you. The site has to answer both without being asked.

01

Make booking as fast as calling

Phone number and booking request in reach on every page, not buried on a contact page. Booking a car should never take more taps than opening the app they were about to open instead.

02

Say what you actually run

Local journeys, airport transfers, advance bookings, larger vehicles, accounts. A passenger who cannot tell whether you cover their journey will assume you do not.

03

Read as an established local firm

Licensing, coverage, hours and a real presence behind the phone number. This is what a national app cannot offer and what a first-time passenger is quietly checking for.

Questions

Straight answers

Can a local firm really compete with the national apps?

Not on app-store presence or paid advertising, and it would be dishonest to suggest otherwise. On local search it is a genuine contest, because a passenger searching for a taxi in your town is expressing a preference for a local option and the apps are not especially well placed for that query.

The firms that win it are the ones that are actually findable and actually bookable in seconds.

Do I need an app?

Almost never, and we will usually talk you out of one. An app has to be found, downloaded and kept, which is three barriers before the first fare. A fast site that books a car in two taps clears all three, and it is a fraction of the cost.

Where an app earns its place is a large account base making repeat bookings, and that is a conversation worth having on its own terms.

Can it connect to my dispatch or booking system?

Often, and how well depends entirely on the system. Some expose a proper integration; some only offer a booking form that hands over to their own flow. We check what yours supports and tell you what is realistic before you commit to it.

Should the phone number be more prominent than online booking?

For most firms, both, in reach at all times. A meaningful share of passengers — and particularly older passengers and airport bookings — still want to speak to someone, and forcing them through a form to prove a point costs you fares.

The site should not have an opinion about how someone gets in touch.

How long does it take?

Typically two to four weeks depending on how many services and areas need covering. Booking integrations are scoped alongside. You get a timeline with the quote, before anything begins.
