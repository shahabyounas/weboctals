The whole area-page argument, in one sentence

Local search

Why the LS postcode keeps costing Leeds businesses work

Three boundaries govern whether you show up locally — the postcode, the council area, and the distance from whoever is searching. In Leeds none of the three line up, and most sites are built as though they do.

01

The postcode is not the council area

LS29 is Ilkley and Burley in Wharfedale, and both sit in Bradford district, not Leeds. LS21 is Otley, which is Leeds. Two neighbouring LS postcodes, two different councils, two different sets of local competitors. Build your area pages on the towns and the councils, never on the postcode prefix.

02

Distance is measured from the searcher, not from you

Google names distance as one of the three things that decide local results, alongside relevance and prominence. It is measured from wherever the person is standing. A profile pinned near the centre is thirteen miles from a Wetherby customer, and thirteen miles is enough to lose the map pack entirely, however good the site is.

03

Nobody searches for the district they live in

People search the name of the place they would say out loud — Horsforth, Morley, Garforth, Guiseley. “Leeds” is what they type when they want the city centre or when they are travelling in. If your towns are only mentioned in a footer list, they are not on the page as far as search is concerned.

04

Service areas beat a shopfront you do not have

If you travel to customers, Google’s guidance is to hide the address and define service areas instead. Draw those on the towns and councils you actually work in, then have the site say the same thing. A profile claiming the whole of West Yorkshire while the site names one city is a mismatch, and mismatches are what quietly hold a listing back.

The fix is boring and it works: name the places you genuinely serve, give each one a page with something real on it, and make your Google Business Profile agree with the site.

Questions

Straight answers

Are you actually based in Leeds?

Yes. WebOctals is a Leeds business, in West Yorkshire. We work as a service-area business, so there is no shopfront and no street address published on the site — the registered address sits with Google and stays hidden, which is what Google recommends when you travel to customers rather than the other way round.

In practice that means a call for most projects and a meeting in person where it genuinely helps, anywhere in the district or the wider county.

Do you have Leeds clients yet?

Not yet. The work so far has been in North London, Harrow, the West Midlands and Bedford, and it is all named and checkable on the case studies. Being straight about that seems better than implying a Yorkshire client list that does not exist.

What travels is the work itself: the same booking flows, ordering systems and local-search structure, built for a business a few miles away instead of a few hundred.

Which towns should my Leeds business target?

The ones you would actually drive to on a Tuesday. For most local businesses that is between three and eight places, each with a page carrying something specific — what you do there, how long you have done it, what a job in that town normally involves.

A shorter list with real content in each beats a long list of near-identical pages every time. Search engines treat the long list as thin, and customers see through it just as fast.

How should a Leeds service business set up its Google Business Profile?

Verify with the real address, then hide it and switch to service areas. Draw those on the towns and councils you work in — Leeds, and Otley or Wetherby or Morley as the case may be — rather than on a radius or on a postcode range.

Then make the website agree with it. The profile and the site naming the same places is the single cheapest local-search improvement available to most businesses, and it costs nothing but an afternoon.

What does a website cost?

Websites are priced by page count: £1,000 up to five pages, £1,500 up to ten, and £2,000+ for twenty or more. Anything not measured in pages — an AI agent, an automation, a change to a site that already exists — is £50 an hour.

All of it is negotiable and all of it is fixed in writing before work starts. The full breakdown, including what counts as a page, is on the pricing page.

Can you guarantee I’ll rank first for “web design Leeds”?

No, and treat anyone who does as a warning sign — Google’s own guidance on hiring an SEO lists a guaranteed number one ranking as a reason to walk away. “Web design Leeds” is one of the most contested commercial phrases in the north, and it is rarely where a local business’s enquiries come from anyway.

What we can do is structural: a page for each area you genuinely serve, service-area markup that matches your Google Business Profile, a site that loads fast on mobile data, and internal links that let all of it be found. That is what makes a ranking reachable. The rest is Google’s call.

How long does a build take?

A typical small business website runs two to four weeks depending on scope. Agents and automation are scoped case by case. You get a timeline alongside the quote, before anything begins.

Search visibility runs on a separate and slower clock, because new pages have to be crawled and indexed before they can rank, and nobody can put a date on that.
