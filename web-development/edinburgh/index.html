Web development in Edinburgh

What it costs

We are a Leeds studio. There is no Edinburgh office, no Scottish staff and no Scottish client yet, and it seems better to put that in the first paragraph than to bury it.

What does carry across the border is the work: booking systems, ordering, agents, automation, and local search structure for a business that serves a defined patch. None of that is different in Scotland, and the rates on this page are the ones every client pays.

A few things genuinely are different, and a studio that has never thought about them will get them wrong — the legal basis of your terms, the domains available to you, and the fact that the EH postcode covers four councils rather than one. Each is dealt with below.

Half the businesses with an Edinburgh postcode are not in the City of Edinburgh council area. If your area pages are built on the postcode, half your coverage is aimed at the wrong council.

The thing that catches people out here

Coverage

The city, the Lothians, and across the bridge

The City of Edinburgh is one council of about 500,000 people. The EH postcode reaches well past it, into three more councils and most of the way to Falkirk. Both facts matter for how a site is built.

City of EdinburghEH1–EH17

Old Town, New Town, Leith, Stockbridge, Morningside, Bruntsfield, Marchmont, Newington, Portobello, Corstorphine, Gorgie, Colinton, Cramond, Liberton, Sighthill, Granton.

The council itself. Dense, walkable and the only part of the region where “Edinburgh” is what a local customer would actually type.

MidlothianEH18–EH20, EH22–EH26

Dalkeith, Bonnyrigg, Penicuik, Loanhead, Gorebridge, Newtongrange, Roslin, Mayfield, Danderhall.

A separate council directly south of the city, with EH postcodes throughout and its own local search market.

East LothianEH21, EH31–EH42

Musselburgh, Tranent, Prestonpans, Haddington, North Berwick, Dunbar, Longniddry, Gullane, Aberlady.

Coastal and commuter, running twenty-five miles east. Musselburgh is EH21 and thoroughly not Edinburgh.

West Lothian and beyondEH27–EH28, EH47–EH55, KY

Livingston, Bathgate, Linlithgow, Broxburn, Armadale, Whitburn; and over the bridge into Dunfermline, Inverkeithing, Kirkcaldy and Rosyth in Fife.

The western corridor and Fife. Livingston is EH54 and fifteen miles out; Fife is a different council area again and a genuinely separate market.

For a business serving the wider central belt, Falkirk, Stirling, Glasgow and the Borders are all within a normal working radius — but each is its own market and each needs naming rather than assuming.

Four councils share the EH postcode. Build your area pages on councils and towns, and the postcode becomes what it should be: an address, not a strategy.

The structural fix

Scotland

What actually changes for a Scottish business

Less than most people expect, and more than nothing. These are the four differences worth building around; everything else works exactly as it does south of the border.

01

Your terms sit under Scots law

Scotland has its own legal system, and website terms, privacy policies and client contracts should say so rather than defaulting to England and Wales. It is a one-line change and it is the sort of detail a prospect notices. We are developers, not solicitors, so we will write the site to match whatever your adviser specifies.

02

.scot is available alongside .co.uk

The .scot domain is open to any business with a Scottish connection, and it reads as local in a way a .com does not. Worth registering next to your .co.uk even if you only redirect it, because the alternative is somebody else holding it.

03

The EH postcode spans four councils

EH21 is Musselburgh, in East Lothian. EH19 is Bonnyrigg, in Midlothian. EH54 is Livingston, in West Lothian. Only EH1 to EH17 are reliably the City of Edinburgh. Draw your Google Business Profile service areas on councils and towns, never on the postcode range.

04

The rest is the same as anywhere

Google ranks a Scottish local business on the same three things it uses everywhere: relevance, distance from the searcher, and prominence. There is no Scottish algorithm and no Scottish trick. Spelling and tone should read as local, and after that it is the ordinary work done properly.

Nothing about the build changes at the border. What changes is the boilerplate, the domain, and the map you draw your service areas on.

Questions

Straight answers

Are you based in Edinburgh?

No. We are a Leeds studio working remotely, and there is no Edinburgh office, no Scottish staff and no Scottish client yet. That is the honest position, and it is better said here than discovered later.

Everything else is checkable: the case studies are named businesses in named towns, and the rates on this page are the ones every client pays.

Does anything actually change for a Scottish business?

Three things. Your terms and privacy policy should sit under Scots law rather than the law of England and Wales; the .scot domain is available to you and is worth holding; and the EH postcode covers four councils, so service areas have to be drawn on councils rather than postcodes.

Beyond that, nothing. The same booking flows, the same ordering systems, the same local search structure, and the same three ranking signals Google uses everywhere.

Should I target Edinburgh or the Lothians?

Both, in the right order. Edinburgh is worth a page because people do search it, but if your customers are in Musselburgh, Dalkeith or Livingston then those towns need pages of their own — a customer in Bathgate is not searching for Edinburgh, and no amount of city-level optimisation will reach them.

The usual answer for a Lothians business is a city page plus three to six town pages, chosen by where the enquiries already come from.

Can you work with a Scottish business from Leeds?

Yes, the same way we work with clients in London, the West Midlands and Bedford. A call, a written scope with the price and timeline fixed before work starts, and direct access to the person writing the code.

If a project genuinely needs meeting in person we will arrange it, but it is rare and it is never the thing that decides whether a build goes well.

What does a website cost?

Websites are priced by page count: £1,000 up to five pages, £1,500 up to ten, and £2,000+ for twenty or more. Anything not measured in pages — an AI agent, an automation, a change to a site that already exists — is £50 an hour.

All of it is negotiable and all of it is fixed in writing before work starts. The full breakdown, including what counts as a page, is on the pricing page.

Can you guarantee I’ll rank first for “web design Edinburgh”?

No, and treat anyone who does as a warning sign — Google’s own guidance on hiring an SEO lists a guaranteed number one ranking as a reason to walk away. Edinburgh is a compact and heavily contested market, and for most local businesses the town-level phrases around it convert better anyway.

What we can do is structural: a page for each area you genuinely serve, service-area markup that matches your Google Business Profile, a site that loads fast on mobile data, and internal links that let all of it be found. That is what makes a ranking reachable. The rest is Google’s call.

How long does a build take?

A typical small business website runs two to four weeks depending on scope. Agents and automation are scoped case by case. You get a timeline alongside the quote, before anything begins.

Search visibility runs on a separate and slower clock, because new pages have to be crawled and indexed before they can rank, and nobody can put a date on that.
