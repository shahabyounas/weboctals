The question to answer before anything is built

Local search

Almost nobody buys a local service by searching “London”

The word is in every brief and almost none of the high-intent queries. Here is where the enquiries actually come from, and how to structure a site to catch them.

01

The intent lives at neighbourhood level

The searches that convert are things like driving instructor in Colindale, barber in Walthamstow, plumber in Balham. They name a place small enough to walk or drive to, because the alternative is a result nine miles and forty minutes away. That is the level your pages have to exist at.

02

“Web design London” is a market, not a keyword

It is one of the most competed commercial phrases in the UK, dominated by agencies with budgets built for exactly that fight. Chasing it is an expensive way to be invisible. The borough and neighbourhood phrases underneath it are reachable, cheaper, and where the customers are.

03

Londoners measure distance in journeys

Nobody says four miles; they say two stops on the Northern line, or the other side of the river. If your service area follows the tube and rail lines rather than a radius, say so in those terms on the page — it is both how customers think and, incidentally, how they search.

04

Structure beats volume

A page for each of the boroughs or neighbourhoods you genuinely work in, each with something specific on it, plus a Google Business Profile whose service areas match. Not a footer list of thirty-two boroughs: that is thin content, and in a market this competitive thin content simply does not surface.

Colindale, Hendon, Edgware, Burnt Oak. Four pages that can be won, instead of one that cannot.

Questions

Straight answers

Are you based in London?

No. We are a Leeds studio working remotely, and there is no London office. London is where most of our work is, though: Colindale Driving School in NW9 and A.R Worldwide in Harrow are both on the case studies, with screenshots and their own pages.

Those clients work with us on a call and a written scope, talking directly to the person building the thing. If a project needs an in-person start we will come down; most do not.

Which part of London do you actually serve?

All of it, in the sense that the work is remote and geography is irrelevant to delivery. The existing work is concentrated in the north and north-west — Barnet, Colindale, Harrow — which is where we know the local search picture best.

For a business anywhere else in the city, nothing about the process or the price changes. The area research is done at the start of the project, for your boroughs.

Should I try to rank for “web design London” or my borough?

Your borough, and then the neighbourhoods inside it. The city-level phrase is contested by firms whose entire budget goes on winning it, and it brings enquiries from all over Greater London, most of which will be outside your realistic catchment anyway.

The borough and neighbourhood phrases are reachable within months rather than years, and the enquiries arrive from people who can actually get to you.

How many London pages should I have?

Fewer than the coverage lists you have seen, and every one of them with something specific on it. For most local London businesses that is between four and ten neighbourhoods, chosen by where the customers already come from rather than by where you would like them to come from.

Look at your last fifty enquiries and count the postcodes. That list, not an ambition, is the sitemap.

What does a website cost?

Websites are priced by page count: £1,000 up to five pages, £1,500 up to ten, and £2,000+ for twenty or more. Anything not measured in pages — an AI agent, an automation, a change to a site that already exists — is £50 an hour.

All of it is negotiable and all of it is fixed in writing before work starts. The full breakdown, including what counts as a page, is on the pricing page.

Can you guarantee I’ll rank first for “web design London”?

No, and treat anyone who does as a warning sign — Google’s own guidance on hiring an SEO lists a guaranteed number one ranking as a reason to walk away. It is plausibly the single most contested commercial phrase in the country, and for a local business it is not where the enquiries are.

What we can do is structural: a page for each area you genuinely serve, service-area markup that matches your Google Business Profile, a site that loads fast on mobile data, and internal links that let all of it be found. That is what makes a ranking reachable. The rest is Google’s call.

How long does a build take?

A typical small business website runs two to four weeks depending on scope. Agents and automation are scoped case by case. You get a timeline alongside the quote, before anything begins.

Search visibility runs on a separate and slower clock, because new pages have to be crawled and indexed before they can rank, and nobody can put a date on that.
