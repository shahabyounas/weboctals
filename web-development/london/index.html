Web development in London

The city where most of our work sits — a driving school in Colindale, a currency exchange in Harrow, and the sort of local trade that lives or dies on a search made on a phone.

What it costs

London is not one market, it is a few hundred high streets that happen to share a transport map. A business in Harrow is not competing with one in Peckham, and a site built around “London” as a single place tends to lose to one built around the three or four areas a business genuinely serves.

That is the part we spend the most time on. A page for each area you actually cover, with valid local markup, beats one homepage trying to name the whole city — and it is the difference between appearing for the searches that bring customers and appearing for none of them.

We work remotely, so there is no London office and nobody will claim one. You talk to the engineer writing the code, which for a project this size is worth more than an address.

A Londoner does not search for a service in London. They search for it in Colindale, or Harrow, or the three streets they are willing to walk. Build for the city and you compete with everyone; build for the catchment and you compete with four people.

Questions

Straight answers

Are you based in London?

No. We are a UK studio working remotely, and we would rather say so on a page with London in the title than let you find out later. There is no London office and no London account manager.

Two of the businesses in the portfolio are London ones — Colindale Driving School in North London and A.R Worldwide in Harrow — so the local work is real even though the desk is not.

Can you compete for “web design London”?

Honestly, that specific term is one of the most contested in the country and we would not promise it to anyone, ourselves included. It is not where the value is.

What is winnable is the narrower search your customers actually make — the trade plus the area, rather than the service plus the capital. That is what we build for.

What does a website cost?

Websites are priced by page count: £1,000 up to five pages, £1,500 up to ten, and £2,000+ for twenty or more. Anything not measured in pages — an AI agent, an automation, a change to a site that already exists — is £50 an hour.

All of it is negotiable and all of it is fixed in writing before work starts. The full breakdown, including what counts as a page, is on the pricing page.

Can you guarantee I’ll rank first for my city?

No, and nobody can. Google’s own guidance on hiring an SEO lists a guaranteed number one ranking as a reason to walk away, and a city as large as this is among the most contested search terms in the country.

What we do is structural: a page for each area you genuinely serve, valid local business markup, a site that is fast on mobile data, and a Google Business Profile that matches. That is what makes a ranking reachable, and it is the honest version of the promise.

How long does a build take?

A typical small business website runs two to four weeks depending on scope. Agents and automation are scoped case by case. You get a timeline alongside the quote, before anything begins.

Search visibility runs on a separate and slower clock, because new pages have to be crawled and indexed first and nobody can put a date on that.
